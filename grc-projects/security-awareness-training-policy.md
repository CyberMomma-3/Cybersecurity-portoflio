## 1. Purpose

The purpose of this policy is to ensure that all workforce members at Bright Smile Family Dentistry receive regular, documented security awareness training that equips them to recognize, avoid, and report cybersecurity threats that could compromise electronic Protected Health Information (ePHI).

Human error is the leading cause of healthcare data breaches. Phishing emails, weak passwords, and unattended workstations are not IT problems — they are people problems that training can prevent. This policy establishes the minimum standards for security awareness training across the entire practice.

> **Regulatory Basis:** The HIPAA Security Rule requires covered entities to implement a security awareness and training program for all workforce members, including management (45 CFR §164.308(a)(5)). Failure to provide documented training is one of the most commonly cited HIPAA violations during OCR audits.

---

## 2. Scope

This policy applies to **all workforce members** of Bright Smile Family Dentistry without exception, including:

- Full-time and part-time employees
- Temporary and contract staff
- Volunteers with access to practice systems
- Dentists, hygienists, and clinical staff
- Administrative and front desk personnel
- Management and practice owners
- **New hires — training must be completed before accessing any practice systems or ePHI**

> **Why Everyone — Including Dentists?** HIPAA defines "workforce" as all employees regardless of role or seniority. A dentist who clicks a phishing link or leaves a workstation unlocked creates the same compliance exposure as any other staff member. No role is exempt.

## 3. Policy Statement

Bright Smile Family Dentistry is committed to protecting the privacy, security, and integrity of patient information. All workforce members share responsibility for maintaining a secure environment. Security awareness training is not optional — it is a condition of employment and a legal requirement under HIPAA.

The practice will provide training that is relevant, practical, and specific to the threats faced by a dental healthcare environment. Training will be updated regularly to reflect the evolving threat landscape, as cyberattack techniques change and improve on a daily basis.

## 4. Training Requirements

| Training Type | Who | When | Frequency |
|--------------|-----|------|-----------|

## 5. Required Training Topics

All annual and onboarding training must cover the following topics at minimum:

### Topic 1 — Phishing Awareness
Recognizing fraudulent emails designed to steal credentials or install malware. How to identify suspicious links, spoofed sender addresses, and urgent language designed to cause panic. What to do — and not do — when a phishing email arrives.

### Topic 2 — Vishing Awareness
Recognizing fraudulent phone calls from attackers posing as vendors, IT support, insurance companies, or other trusted parties. Never provide passwords, system access, or patient information over the phone without verifying the caller's identity through official channels.

### Topic 3 — Physical Security & Tailgating
Preventing unauthorized individuals from following staff into restricted areas of the practice. All visitors must be escorted. Staff are empowered to politely challenge anyone they do not recognize in non-public areas of the office.

### Topic 4 — Clean Desk & Clean Screen
Patient schedules, records, and personal information must never be visible to other patients or unauthorized individuals. Computer screens at the front desk must be positioned or filtered to prevent patient information from being seen from the waiting area.

### Topic 5 — Document Disposal & Shredding
Any document containing patient information must be shredded using a cross-cut shredder. Documents must never be placed in regular trash or recycling bins. Shredding bins must be emptied regularly by the authorized shredding vendor.

### Topic 6 — Password Security
Creating strong passwords of at least 12 characters combining letters, numbers, and symbols. Passwords must never be shared with coworkers, written on sticky notes, or reused across multiple systems. Each staff member is responsible for their own login credentials.

### Topic 7 — Workstation Locking & Logoff
Staff must lock their workstation or log out whenever stepping away — even briefly. A patient's information must never be left visible on screen when moving to a different patient or leaving the work area. Computers must be fully logged off at the end of each shift.

### Topic 8 — Incident Recognition & Reporting
Recognizing warning signs of a security incident including unusual pop-ups, unresponsive computers, unexpected password prompts, or suspicious emails. Staff must report anything unusual to the Office Manager immediately — never attempt to investigate or fix it independently.

### Topic 9 — Malware & Ransomware Awareness
Understanding what ransomware is, how it spreads, and what it looks like when it strikes. The single most important rule: **do not click anything on an affected computer.** Disconnect from the network and notify the Office Manager immediately.

## 6. Training Delivery Methods

| Method | Description | Best Used For |
|--------|------------|--------------|
| In-Person Group Training | Instructor-led session with all staff present | Annual refresher, post-incident training |
| Online Training Platform | Self-paced modules with quiz completion tracking (e.g., KnowBe4, Proofpoint) | Onboarding, annual refresher |
| Simulated Phishing Exercise | Practice sends a fake phishing email to test staff recognition | Ongoing awareness reinforcement |
| Written Materials + Acknowledgment | Policy documents with signed acknowledgment of receipt | Policy updates, supplemental training |

> **Best Practice:** Combine at least two methods annually — for example, an online training module followed by a simulated phishing exercise 30 days later.

## 7. Documentation and Recordkeeping

HIPAA requires that training be documented. **If it isn't documented, it didn't happen.**

### Required Documentation for Every Training Session
- [ ] Date training was conducted
- [ ] Topics covered and training materials used
- [ ] Name of trainer or training platform
- [ ] Full name of each participant
- [ ] Participant signature or electronic acknowledgment
- [ ] Security Officer or Office Manager signature

### Recordkeeping Requirements
- Training records must be retained for a minimum of **6 years** per HIPAA requirements
- Records must be stored securely in a locked physical file or secured digital system
- Records must be accessible to the Security Officer and Practice Owner at any time
- Training Completion Log must be updated after every training session

## 8. Enforcement and Disciplinary Action

Completion of security awareness training is a condition of employment. Failure to complete required training will result in progressive disciplinary action:

**Step 1 — Reminder and Final Deadline**
Office Manager issues a written reminder with a firm completion deadline of 5 business days. System access may be temporarily restricted until training is completed.

**Step 2 — Formal Written Warning**
A formal written warning is issued and placed in the employee's personnel file, stating that further non-compliance may result in termination.

**Step 3 — Disciplinary Action Up To and Including Termination**
Continued non-compliance places the practice at direct HIPAA liability. Termination may be pursued at the Practice Owner's discretion. All steps must be documented prior to termination.

> **Zero Tolerance:** Any workforce member who intentionally circumvents security controls, shares passwords, or deliberately ignores a known security threat may be subject to immediate termination, bypassing the progressive discipline process.
